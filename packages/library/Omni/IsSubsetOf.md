93. Omni :: Is Subset Of (Logic)
Who is it? The Paradox. The Error. The Impossible. Wait—how can the Everything be a subset of Logic? It can't.
What is it? The Categorical Failure. This relationship highlights a limit. Logic tries to put the Omni in a box (a subset). But the Omni contains the box. Therefore, from the perspective of Logic, the Omni appears as a "Subset" called "Undefined" or "Infinity." It is the glitch in the matrix.
When is it? When you try to prove God with math. When you try to define the Tao with words.
Where is it? In the Blind Spot. It is where Logic breaks.
Why is it? Because Logic is Finite. Logic requires definitions (boundaries). The Omni has no boundaries. Therefore, Logic can only capture a subset of the Omni—the part that follows rules. The rest escapes.
How does it operate? Through Incompleteness. As Gödel proved, no logical system can contain all truths. The Omni is the Truth that lies outside the system.
Ontological Phenomena Overview: This is the phenomenon of Logical Singularity. It reveals that Reason is a tool for navigating the finite world, but it is insufficient for grasping the Infinite. The map is smaller than the territory.
